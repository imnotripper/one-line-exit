# Candidate Selection — The One-Line Exit

## The brief
One line, 1–140 characters, reads like a resignation letter a person could
actually send. The joke must land inside that single line. Judge criteria, in
order: (1) funny one-line resignation letter; (2) SURPRISE — the turn arrives
somewhere unexpected, not the usual reasons people quit; (3) fits the form —
one line, no padding, no list of grievances, sendable; (4) specific voice —
a recognizable job/workplace/person behind it; (5) stays with the reader —
quotable, repeatable, forwardable.

Angle: resignation-letter shape with ONE specific workplace grievance; the
punchline turns on the form itself (the letter's own brevity) or on one
concrete office absurdity.

## Elimination rounds
- Round 1 (criterion 1, funny one-liner): cut #1, #4, #6, #8 (scores 3, jokes
  land soft or read as a complaint with a shrug).
- Round 2 (criterion 2, surprise): cut #3, #5, #7, #11 (solid jokes, but the
  turn is a familiar office trope — plant, unmute, sync, "family" — seen
  before).
- Round 3 (criteria 3–5, form/voice/stickiness): #2 vs #9 vs #10 vs #12.
  #2's "promoted to customer" is sharp but its second clause explains the
  joke; #9's "circled out the door" is good but HR jargon narrows the
  audience; #10 is vivid but "professional napper" is a fantasy job, slightly
  less sendable as a real letter. #12 wins: the joke is the letter itself.

## Winner: #12
> This is my two weeks' notice, condensed to one line because, like my raise, it was always going to be shorter than promised.

Char count: 124 (verified by node; limit 140).

## Winner's rationale
The surprise is structural, not topical: the line apologises for its own
brevity and then blames the missing raise for it — the grievance (no raise)
IS the reason the letter is one line, so form and punchline are the same
joke. It reads exactly like something a real employee would send, it needs
no setup, and the final clause ("shorter than promised") is the quotable
part a colleague forwards. No padding, no list of grievances, one specific
voice (the underpaid employee), one line.
